# Facebook page + Meta ads — launch pack

Everything to set up the page and run the first ads. Copy is written to be
pasted as-is. Nothing here claims a customer we don't have — the honest hook is
**3 free searches, no card**, and that is what every piece leads with.

---

## 0. First decision: one name or two?

The ad, the page, the website and the payment page should all say the same word.
Right now the page is **Easy Freelancing** and the product is **EasyClient**.
A stranger who taps an ad, lands on a page called one thing, clicks through to a
site called another, then sends ₱999 to a third name will hesitate — and in this
market hesitation is the whole ballgame.

**Recommended:** rename the page to **EasyClient**. Facebook allows renaming and
keeps your existing followers and likes. Settings → Page setup → Name.

Keeping "Easy Freelancing" is defensible if the page already has real reach — in
that case put `EasyClient` in the first line of the About so the connection is
explicit before anyone clicks.

The rest of this document assumes the page is **EasyClient**.

## 4. Meta ads

### Before you spend a peso: the Special Ad Category trap

Meta restricts ads *about job opportunities* to the **Employment** special ad
category, which strips out most targeting — no age, no gender, no detailed
interests, and a minimum 15-mile location radius.

EasyClient is a **tool**, not a job posting, so it should not need that
category. But Meta's automated review does not always draw the line carefully,
and ad copy that reads like *"apply to these jobs"* invites the wrong
classification.

Write the ads as **software that saves you time**, never as *"we have jobs for
you"*. If an ad is rejected or forced into Employment, appeal once, then reword
toward the tool angle rather than fighting it.

### Campaign settings

| Setting | Value |
|---|---|
| Objective | **Traffic** to start (switch to Conversions once you have 50+ signups) |
| Budget | ₱150–300/day. Below ~₱150 Meta can't learn; above ₱300 you're guessing expensively |
| Location | Philippines |
| Age | 22–45 |
| Placement | Advantage+ (automatic). Facebook Feed and Reels will take most of it |
| Destination | Your domain, **not** the Facebook page |

Targeting: start with **broad** — Philippines, 22–45, no interests. Meta's
algorithm finds freelancers faster than an interest list you guessed at. If you
want a second ad set to compare, try interests: *Upwork, Freelancer.com,
OnlineJobs.ph, Virtual assistant, Work from home*.

Run **3 ads at once**, same budget, and let them compete for 4–5 days before
judging anything.

### What the numbers should look like

At ₱200/day in the Philippines, roughly: 8,000–15,000 people reached,
100–250 link clicks, CPC around ₱1–3.

Watch **cost per signup**, not clicks. At ₱999/month, a customer who stays three
months is worth ~₱3,000, so you can afford ₱200–400 to acquire one and still be
well ahead. If cost per signup climbs past ~₱500, pause and change the ad before
adding budget.

Give any ad at least 4 days and ₱800 before killing it. Meta's learning phase is
real, and day-one numbers are noise.

## 5. Messenger scripts

Payment runs through DMs, so these are the actual sales process. Keep them
close, but rewrite each one slightly — copy-paste that reads like a bot loses
the sale.

**"Magkano?"**

    ₱999/month po — 20 searches a day sa tatlong site.

    Pero libre po muna: 3 searches, walang card, walang hihinging payment
    details. Subukan niyo muna: easyclientph.com

    Kung gusto niyo na, dito lang po ako.

**"Legit ba ito?" / "Scam ba?"**

    Fair na tanong po — ang dami talagang scam sa online jobs.

    Kaya ganito: 3 free searches muna, walang card at walang payment info na
    hinihingi. Bawat trabaho sa results ay naka-link sa totoong post sa
    OnlineJobs, LinkedIn o Upwork — buksan niyo, i-check niyo mismo.

    Hindi rin po namin hinihingi ang password niyo kahit kailan.

    Bago pa po kami, wala pa kaming ipapakitang mahabang listahan ng customers —
    yung free searches na lang po muna ang tingnan niyo.

> That last line matters. Being new is not a weakness you hide; pretending
> otherwise is what actually gets you caught.

**"Paano magbayad?"**

    Dito po lahat ng details: easyclientph.com/get-access

    GCash, BPI o GoTyme — may QR code po doon.

    Pagkabayad, i-send niyo lang po dito ang screenshot ng receipt at ang email
    address na gagamitin niyo. Yung email po ang gagamitin namin para i-on ang
    account niyo, kaya pakisigurado ang spelling.

**Receipt received**

    Salamat po! Nakita ko na ang payment.

    I-o-on ko na po ang account niyo ngayon — mga ilang minuto lang. Kung may
    account na kayo, mag-sign in ulit at bukas na po ang LinkedIn at Upwork.
    Kung wala pa, may email po kayong matatanggap para gumawa ng password.

**After activating**

    Okay na po! Naka-full access na ang [email].

    20 searches a day sa tatlong site. Message niyo lang po ako kung may hindi
    gumagana.

**Renewal, sent 3 days before the month ends**

    Hi po! Malapit na pong matapos ang buwan niyo sa EasyClient (until [date]).

    Kung gusto niyong ituloy, ₱999 lang po ulit sa parehong GCash/BPI/GoTyme,
    tapos send niyo lang ang receipt dito.

    Kung hindi muna — okay lang po, hihinto lang siya. Walang auto-charge,
    walang cancellation fee.

> Renewals are manual, so this message *is* your recurring revenue. Keep a
> simple sheet: email, date paid, date due. Miss the reminder and you lose the
> customer without ever knowing why.

**Unhappy customer**

    Sorry po talaga. Ano pong nangyari? Gusto kong malaman kung bug ba ito o
    kulang ang tool.

    Kung hindi po kayo nakakuha ng value ngayong buwan, ibabalik ko po ang ₱999.
    Sabihin niyo lang.

> Refund the first few complaints without arguing. One angry public comment on
> a new page costs more than ₱999.
